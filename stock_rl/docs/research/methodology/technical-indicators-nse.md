# Technical Indicators on NSE — What Has Evidence

Note on method: `websearch` returned zero results throughout this review.
All findings came from OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, Crossref, arXiv and
Wikipedia APIs, plus a text proxy for paywalled full texts.

## Executive summary

1. **Exactly one peer-reviewed, cost-aware Indian study of moving-average
   rules exists: Mitra (2011), *Quantitative Finance*.** It finds MA rules
   earn positive gross returns but *"these returns cannot be exploited
   fully due to real world transaction costs."* Its open-access companion
   contains the full breakeven-cost table — the most useful single artefact
   for this design.
2. **The 50/200 crossover is folklore in India.** No published study tests
   it on NSE/Nifty data. Longest horizons ever tested: 60-day SMA, 199-day
   EMA, 150-day (BRICS 2016).
3. **The cost-viable zone is 20–60 day single moving averages, or a
   ~12/20-day EMA crossover** — roughly 5–20 trades per year.
4. **Momentum is the best-evidenced family in India**, replicated four times
   across three institutions, and the strongest of the five BRICS markets.
5. **Rink (2023): with 6,406 rules and a stepwise Superior Predictive
   Ability test, best-in-sample rules underperform buy-and-hold
   out-of-sample at 25–50 bp.** India is not in his sample, but every
   emerging market in it turned unpredictable in 2009–2016.
6. **ADX, OBV, VWAP, ATR-as-signal, MACD, India-VIX term structure and RSI
   have essentially no Indian equity evidence. Drop them.**
7. **Camilleri & Green (2014): observed NSE index predictability is better
   explained by non-synchronous trading than by inefficiency.** Any
   index-level signal may be a stale-price artifact.

## Tier 1 — Indian, peer-reviewed, cost-aware

| Study | Sample | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **Mitra (2011)**, *Quant. Finance* 11(2) | 4 Indian index series | MA rules "capture the direction of market movements reasonably well... **But these returns cannot be exploited fully due to real world transaction costs**" |
| **Mitra (2011)**, *Int. J. Business & Management* 6(7) | Nifty + Junior, Dec 2000–Nov 2010, 2,497 days | Breakeven-cost table below. Reality-check p-values reject the null at 1% **only at zero cost** |
| **Bhama (2025)**, *Investment Mgmt & Financial Innovations* 22(4) | NSE 500, 27 yrs, SMA/EMA at 5/10/20/50/100d, **beta deciles, with costs** | Mid-beta: alpha 7–14% gross; "transaction costs erode much of the excess returns generated by **shorter-lag** strategies"; 20d and 50d retain 6–11% net alpha |
| **Gunasekarage & Power (2001)**, *Emerging Markets Review* 2(1) | BSE, CSE, Dhaka, Karachi, 1990–2000 | "Technical trading rules have predictive ability in these markets... generates excess returns to investors in South Asian markets" — **gross of costs** |
| **Sobreiro et al. (2016)**, *NAJEF* 38 | 4,021 stocks, 9 markets; India 756 | India: 78.3% beat buy-and-hold with SMA-SMA — but India was one of only 4 markets where SMA-SMA did *not* dominate risk-adjusted. **EMA-EMA best for India** |
| **Souza et al. (2018)**, *Financial Innovation* 4(3) | 4,428 MA strategies, 0/2%/5% costs; India 755 | India and Russia highest average returns, but "**few combinations of moving averages were able to outperform... buy and hold**". ## Indian momentum evidence — strongest in the set

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Maheshwari & Dhankar (2017)**, *J. Advances in Mgmt Research* | 470 BSE stocks, 1997–2013. Profitable; survives size, value, illiquidity controls; **asymmetric, driven by winners**, so the long leg works without shorting |
| Same (2017), *Vision / J. Business Perspective* | "Momentum returns... turn **negative during crisis periods**" |
| **Mohapatra & Misra (2019)**, *IIMB Management Review* 21(4) | Momentum profits exist; drivers are P/E, P/B and net FII inflows |
| **Barik & Balakrishnan (2022)**, *IIMB Management Review* 24(2) | Idiosyncratic volatility significantly affects momentum, short and long term |
| *Accounting analyses of momentum and contrarian strategies in emerging markets* (2017), *Asia-Pacific J. Acct & Economics* | **"The Indian market has the strongest momentum effect"** of the five BRICS |
| **Singh, Walia, Bekiros & Gupta**, *JEFAS* / *Copernican J.* | BSE monthly 1996–2020. TS momentum significant; **TS > cross-sectional**, driven by the net-long leg |
| **Sehgal & Balakrishnan (2002)**, *Vikalpa* 27(1) | Short-term **continuation** → significantly positive payoffs. Long-term reversal only with a **1-year gap** between formation and holding |

| Indicator | Status |
|---|---|

| **Bollinger Bands** | Best "Indian" study is 8 months of hourly data on 14 stocks. Not a production basis |
| **ATR as a signal** | Zero evidence of directional predictive power anywhere. It is a sizing tool |
| **India VIX term structure** | Indian literature *forecasts VIX* rather than using it to time equities. Best directional use: VIX-direction ≈ **53.1%** accuracy |
| **52-week high** | Raju (2023) exists but is a non-peer-reviewed working paper; Bornholt & Malin show it strictly dominated by plain momentum |

## Mitra's breakeven-cost table — the single most useful artefact

Nifty, Dec 2000–Nov 2010, long+short, **gross**:

| Rule | Ann. return | Trades/yr | **Breakeven one-way cost** | Viable at ~22 bps delivery? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SMA(3) | 28.56% | 86.2 | 0.15% | No |
| SMA(5) | 32.35% | 54.1 | 0.30% | No |
| SMA(10) | 29.00% | 34.7 | 0.41% | No |
| **SMA(20)** | 31.04% | 20.2 | **0.72%** | Marginal |
| **SMA(40)** | 26.65% | 12.8 | **0.91%** | **Yes** |
| **SMA(60)** | 25.74% | 14.2 | **1.24%** | **Yes** |
| EMA α=.30 & .15 (~6d/12d) | **−13.07%** | 4.3 | negative | **Loses money** |
| EMA α=.30 & .05 (~6d/40d) | **−7.67%** | 2.5 | negative | **Loses money** |
| EMA α=.20 & .05 (~9d/40d) | 18.85% | 4.4 | 1.59% | Yes |
| EMA α=.20 & .01 (~9d/**199d**) | 12.27% | 6.5 | 1.69% | Yes |
| **EMA α=.15 & .10 (~12d/19d)** | **25.96%** | **5.8** | **1.73%** | **Best** |
| EMA α=.15 & .01 (~12d/**199d**) | 14.44% | 6.5 | 2.27% | Yes |

**Three defensible conclusions:**

1. **Short-term MA crossovers are actively harmful in Indian data.** The
   6d/12d and 6d/40d EMA pairs lost 13% and 7.7% annualised *before any
   costs*. This is measured damage, not folklore.
2. **The best risk/cost-adjusted crossover is ~12-day EMA vs ~20-day
   EMA.** Note `α = 0.10` corresponds to a 19-day EMA under `N = 2/α − 1`.
   Close to the retail "20/50" intuition but materially faster.
3. **A ~200-day EMA *was* tested** (α=0.01 ≈ 199-day), paired with 9- or
   12-day EMAs. The pairing tested was **12/200, not 50/200.** Do not
   claim 50/200; there is no data.

## Time frame

**Daily bars, 20–60 day lookback, 5–20 trades per year.**

Mitra prices this directly: *"The shorter the moving average, the more
number of trades and increases trading cost... Individual investors to
minimize his frequency of trading may prefer longer moving averages."*

- **Intraday: no credible Indian evidence.** The only hourly study covers 8
  months on 14 stocks.
- **Weekly: effectively untested on Indian data.**
- **Monthly: only for cross-sectional momentum**, which is a stock-ranking
  construct, not index timing.

## Regime and conditioning

- **Predictability decays with time.** Rink (2023): of 14 emerging markets
  predictable over the full sample, *"in the last subperiod between 2009
  and 2016, almost all emerging market indices are unpredictable."*
  **India is one of the markets in that sample** (Table 3, Panel B: `IND`,
  BSE Sensex), so this is a statement about Indian data and not a general
  emerging-markets caveat. An earlier version of this file claimed
  otherwise.
- **Momentum inverts in crises.** Maheshwari & Dhankar: positive
  pre-crisis, **negative during**, positive post.
- **Condition on volatility, not on a trend/sideways label.** Han, Yang &
  Zhou (2013), *JFQA* 48(5): MA timing works on **high-volatility**
  portfolios and fails on low-volatility ones. ADX as a trend/range
  classifier has no supporting evidence.
- **Structural breaks induce mean reversion** in India, with break dates
  tied to the rupee, oil shocks and the subprime crisis.

## Sector and beta

- **Bhama (2025)**: *"technical strategies are most effective in beta
  portfolios with moderate systematic risk exposure."* Mid-beta 7–14%
  gross, 6–11% net at 20d/50d. High-beta does not carry the edge.
- **Large caps are the least predictable Indian segment.** The
  variance-ratio study (2012): *"Rejection of random walk is relatively
  stronger for smaller and medium indices than larger indices implying
  that market capitalization and liquidity play a greater role in improving
  the efficiency of the market."*

**Uncomfortable implication:** Nifty-50 constituents are exactly the
most-efficient names. **Weak signals on large caps are the expected
result, not a bug.**

**Recommendation:** compute signals on **market-excess returns**, not raw
prices, and report performance by beta decile. This protects against the
criticism that a "signal" is just levered market beta. Separate the long
and short legs — India's momentum is winner-driven, so a long-short Sharpe
overstates what a long-only delivery book can harvest.

## Mixed-indicator strategies

The user asked specifically about combining indicators. The evidence
constrains this more than it enables it:

- **The only strong regime-conditional evidence is volatility-sorted, not
  trend-classified.** Han, Yang & Zhou apply a trend-timing overlay to
  portfolios sorted on volatility. That is a *filter on a sorted
  universe*, not a blend of indicators.
- **Volume as a conditioning variable has evidence; OBV as a signal does
  not.** Maheshwari & Dhankar (2017), *Global Business Review*: heavy
  trading stocks earn higher momentum *and* contrarian returns in India.
  Bornholt, Dou & Malin (2015), 37 countries: volume-based early-stage
  momentum beats traditional momentum in **34 of 37**. Use volume to tilt
  momentum, not as OBV.
- **Combined gross profitability + momentum** has strong evidence — but
  that is a *fundamental* factor combination from Fama-French, not
  technical indicators. See [fundamentals-nse.md](fundamentals-nse.md).
- **No published Indian study tests a multi-indicator technical blend.**
  Rink's 6,406 rules were tested individually with a stepwise SPA test;
  a hand-picked blend is precisely the kind of ex-post construction
  Park & Irwin (2007) identify as the source of positive-but-bogus
  technical results.
- **In India, low-volatility and quality are partly the same signal.**
  Low-vol stocks have higher profitability; LV partially survives
  controlling for profitability. Building both is two bets, not three.

**Practical conclusion:** blend indicators only where the combination has
independent evidence, and prefer an *ensemble of decorrelated signals over
equal weights* to a single blended score. Multi-agent debate research
found the same mechanism: value comes from *"preserving independent
informational signals across agents"*, not from the combination step.

## Skeptical position — the strongest version

> "Six thousand four hundred and six technical rules were tested on 41
> national equity indices with a state-of-the-art multiple-hypothesis test.
> Technical rules had predictive power in-sample in most emerging markets —
> but by 2009–2016 almost all had lost it. Selecting the best rules on a
> rolling 3-year window and trading the next 3 years produced returns that
> were *insignificant* at zero costs and **significantly negative** at 25
> and 50 basis points. For the emerging-market portfolio the out-of-sample
> excess Sharpe was −0.507. Bajgrowicz and Scaillet independently showed
> that even in-sample DJIA profits are entirely consumed by low
> transaction costs. Fang, Jacobsen and Qin found zero predictive power
> out-of-sample. Heyman et al. found significant profits in 4 of 34
> emerging markets. On Indian data specifically, Mitra showed MA profits
> vanish after costs, Souza et al. found few MA combinations beat
> buy-and-hold, and Camilleri and Green showed observed NSE predictability
> is better explained by stale prices than inefficiency. **We conclude
> technical trading signals should be treated as noise.**"

This is a fair characterisation. The burden of proof is on us.

**CORRECTION (2026-10): this paragraph was wrong.** It previously said
Rink's sample *excludes* India. It does not: his Table 3, Panel B lists
`IND`, the BSE Sensex, over 03/04/1979–31/05/2016. So his subperiod finding
below — that emerging markets became unpredictable in 2009–2016 — **applies
to India directly**, and the caveat that protected this project from it was
void. Two things follow, and they do not cancel:

- The burden is now *heavier*, not lighter. Rink tested 6,406 rules against
  India with a stepwise SPA test, and a rule that is not significantly
  positive out-of-sample after costs is not an edge in Indian data.
- Rink's own framing is still adaptive-markets: profitability decays *with
  competition*, which argues for slow-moving, low-turnover rules — exactly
  what survives Indian costs — rather than abandoning signals outright.

His India-specific rule count could not be isolated from the paper, so this
project does not claim a number for it. See
[indicator-trend-strategies.md](indicator-trend-strategies.md), which reaches
the compatible conclusion from the other direction: Indian momentum is
replicated across a dozen groups GROSS of costs, and absent NET.

## Non-negotiable validation

1. **Report breakeven cost per rule, Mitra-style.** Machine-checkable. If
   breakeven < ~0.3% per one-way trade, the rule is dead on arrival.
2. **Run White's Reality Check or Hansen SPA** on the full signal library.
   Souza et al. ran 4,428 MA combinations and Sobreiro et al. 1,581 × 3;
   neither applied a multiple-testing correction.
3. **Walk-forward.** Rink's design: select on 36 months, trade the next 36.
   **If OOS beats in-sample, you have a bug.**
4. **Test at 25 bp and 50 bp single-trip.** Rink's best rules die there.
5. **Check non-synchronous trading.** Test whether an index-level signal
   survives an overnight-return decomposition.
6. **Split long and short legs.**
7. **Report per-beta-decile results.** Weak Nifty-50 mega-cap results are
   expected.

## Limitations of this review

Blocked and unverified: SSRN (`Which Momentum?`, `52-Week High Effect`),
ScienceDirect abstract pages, Taylor & Francis (Mitra's *Quantitative
Finance* full text — the open-access IJBM companion was used for all
tables). Not retrieved: Gunasekarage & Power (2001) full result tables —
abstract and introduction suffice for the qualitative claim.

No Indian study was found for ADX, OBV, VWAP, or VIX-term-structure
timing. That is reported as **absence of evidence**, which is a finding,
but absence in OpenAlex/Crossref is not proof of absence in every Indian
journal. A targeted search of IIM/IIMB/ISI institutional repositories is
the highest-value remaining check.
